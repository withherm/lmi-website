# Lean Methods Institute: external website review

Reviewed 10 September 2026.

The site has a credible point of view, but it asks buyers to trust claims that its own course and assessment pages contradict. The biggest risk is not looking unfinished. It is looking precise until someone checks the detail.

I read the full brief, reviewed the nine URLs grouped under its core pages on desktop and at 390-pixel mobile width, and followed the navigation, government enquiry and Green Belt enrolment journeys. I also inspected supporting resource, method and course pages, and the EQUIBT homepage for the group comparison. This is a focused commercial review, not an exhaustive review of every route. Findings are ordered by my judgement of likely commercial cost, not measured conversion loss. No enquiries were submitted.

**1. The credential promise changes depending on where the buyer reads it.**

**Where:** [Green Belt](https://6b959d5a-lmi-website.equibt.workers.dev/courses/lean-six-sigma-green-belt/), hero and assessment; [Certifications](https://6b959d5a-lmi-website.equibt.workers.dev/certifications/), four gates and project-return example; [In-house](https://6b959d5a-lmi-website.equibt.workers.dev/in-house/), Deliver and certification confidence.

**What is wrong:** The site sells one apparently universal, independently assessed credential journey, then describes materially different conditions and completion points.

Green Belt promises the credential “in five days”, but its assessment section requires a workplace project, typically within six months. In-house says participants leave with a credential and a result in progress. Certifications presents a completed measured project as a gate to the credential. These are different purchase promises.

The universal claims also fail at the edges. Certifications says there is no participation certificate, while White Belt explicitly offers a completion certificate without assessment. In-house says every credential is independently assured, while the Zero Defects Practitioner page says its certification arrangements are unsettled. A reader should not have to infer the exceptions.

The project-return example is particularly damaging: nothing was measured before the change, but the credential follows after the baseline is supplied. Historical records might support a defensible reconstruction. If no valid historical evidence exists, measuring later does not recreate the original baseline. The page does not make that distinction.

**Why it costs:** Procurement cannot confidently record what is being bought. A practitioner cannot plan when they will qualify. A quality leader may question the assessment standard precisely where the site is trying hardest to establish rigour.

**What I would do:** Establish the actual requirements for each credential with the relevant assessing body, then reconcile every summary against them. Put teaching duration, project period, award conditions and included coaching together beside the fee. Restrict the four-gate model to the credentials it actually governs. Replace the missing-baseline example with an accurate account of acceptable evidence and what happens when it cannot be recovered. Link to the relevant body's published assessment and verification information where available. I am not alleging that independence is false; the website does not substantiate its scope adequately.

**2. The cohort offer gives a sponsor a philosophy, but too little to get the purchase approved.**

**Where:** [In-house](https://6b959d5a-lmi-website.equibt.workers.dev/in-house/), “Three steps, no guesswork”, AI advantage and testimonials; [Government](https://6b959d5a-lmi-website.equibt.workers.dev/in-house/government/), context and recommended programmes; [Approach](https://6b959d5a-lmi-website.equibt.workers.dev/approach/), cohort sequence.

**What is wrong:** Repeated assurances about real work and contextualisation substitute for the practical engagement definition and evidence a sponsor needs to defend the spend.

Discover, Design and Deliver is a supplier activity list. It does not show the buyer's commitment: project selection, sponsor participation, time away from work, access to usable data, coaching boundaries, assessment milestones or the response to a stalled project. Some information exists elsewhere, but the page selling the largest engagement does not assemble it.

The in-house testimonials support facilitation and learning experience. They do not substantiate the stronger promise that capability and measured results persist. The homepage's longer-term testimonial is relevant, but still cannot show how the work was assessed or sustained.

**Why it costs:** The operations leader must create the internal business case themselves or arrange a call before they can even judge feasibility. That creates work for the person you need as your advocate.

**What I would do:** Make this page a compact approval aid: intended outcome, suitable projects, buyer responsibilities, delivery and coaching commitments, assessment route, and what the quotation includes. Add one genuinely documented, anonymised project example showing the problem, participant work, evidence reviewed and follow-through. Use only substantiated results. Put the outline in a working downloadable document that a sponsor can circulate. The actual delivery model should determine the content, not a generic procurement checklist.

**3. The site's hierarchy treats the main revenue buyer as a secondary visitor.**

**Where:** [Homepage](https://6b959d5a-lmi-website.equibt.workers.dev/), hero, “What we teach” and in-house section; shared mobile Courses menu.

**What is wrong:** The site foregrounds choosing a belt while repeatedly bypassing the page that explains buying a cohort.

In-house is buried beneath method families and public dates in the mobile Courses submenu. The homepage's in-house card sits alongside methods, although delivery format is a different choice, and its “View programs” action leads straight to Contact. The later in-house call to action also goes to Contact. A buyer can repeatedly express cohort interest without seeing the cohort proposition.

**Why it costs:** Your highest-value buyer is asked to enquire before receiving the information that would make the enquiry confident and specific. The generic hero action does not resolve that information gap.

**What I would do:** Yes, promote in-house to the top level. Keep the shared navigation styling, but adapt its contents to this business's buying priorities. Give the homepage an immediately visible route to the cohort offer and a separate route to public courses. Send exploratory in-house cards to the in-house page; retain direct enquiry actions for visitors ready to talk.

This is an active objection to the current hierarchy, not to group consistency. Matching EQUIBT's navigation appearance does not require matching its commercial priorities. Keep White Belt off the main homepage ladder: a free awareness course would further dilute the cohort decision. Make it easy to find within the belt pathway and individual learner journey.

**4. Several calls to action promise an output that the destination does not provide.**

**Where:** [Green Belt](https://6b959d5a-lmi-website.equibt.workers.dev/courses/lean-six-sigma-green-belt/), purchase panel; [Resources](https://6b959d5a-lmi-website.equibt.workers.dev/resources/), featured selector; [Tools](https://6b959d5a-lmi-website.equibt.workers.dev/resources/tools/), readiness check; [Contact](https://6b959d5a-lmi-website.equibt.workers.dev/contact/).

**What is wrong:** Dates, enrolment, a course download and self-service advice are presented as available actions but lead to generic enquiries or descriptions of unfinished tools.

I followed “View dates and enrol” from Green Belt. It opens Contact with in-house training selected, without carrying the course choice. “Download course overview” points to the same page. The selector promoted across the sales pages is a non-interactive description at its destination. The readiness check similarly leads to a tool list.

**Why it costs:** The individual practitioner loses a fast purchase path. Procurement loses the document they wanted to circulate. The hesitant cohort buyer loses the promised way to assess fit privately. Each learns that button labels cannot be taken literally.

**What I would do:** Make each action deliver the stated next step. Until it can, describe the action that is actually available and retain course and delivery intent in the enquiry. Do not promote unavailable tools from sales pages.

On the known resource question: I reject the credibility argument for this roadmap in its present position. An explicitly labelled roadmap is honest, but honesty about an empty destination does not fulfil the promise made before the click. A small useful library is commercially stronger here. The preview also explicitly says the enquiry form is not connected; I have not treated that declared build state as a separate finding.

**5. The AI position has a useful teaching distinction, but no visible demonstration of competence.**

**Where:** [Homepage](https://6b959d5a-lmi-website.equibt.workers.dev/), AI section; [Green Belt](https://6b959d5a-lmi-website.equibt.workers.dev/courses/lean-six-sigma-green-belt/), AI and prerequisites; [AI-Enabled Improvement](https://6b959d5a-lmi-website.equibt.workers.dev/methods/ai-enabled/).

**What is wrong:** The site explains what AI may do and who remains responsible, but does not show how participants learn to recognise an output that should be rejected.

The belt-specific examples are better than a generic AI claim. Choosing the test and owning its assumptions gives the position substance. But “AI removes the grind” and the claim that most training bolts AI on remain assertions. Corporate AI is also a material condition of delivery, yet the course's “None” prerequisite leaves tool access and supported working arrangements unresolved for the buyer.

**Why it costs:** A corporate or government leader needs confidence that the programme produces better judgement in the tools their staff can actually use. Saying a person owns the decision does not demonstrate that the course teaches them to make it well.

**What I would do:** Show one original teaching example: task, AI output, the flaw a learner must identify, the check they perform and the evidence an assessor expects. State the tool access required and the available route for participants without an approved environment. Keep the existing boundary between assistance and human judgement, but substantiate it.

“We build lean, AI-capable teams” is currently a slogan supported by the beginnings of a position. The defensible position is the taught and assessed judgement inside improvement work. Merely including AI is not enough to establish differentiation. That is a judgement of the evidence on this site, not a claim about an unreviewed competitor market.

**6. The method catalogue obscures the choice between overlapping programmes.**

**Where:** [Methods](https://6b959d5a-lmi-website.equibt.workers.dev/methods/), family cards; [Lean Practitioner](https://6b959d5a-lmi-website.equibt.workers.dev/courses/lean-practitioner/), outcomes and assessment; [Certifications](https://6b959d5a-lmi-website.equibt.workers.dev/certifications/), two ladders.

**What is wrong:** The catalogue describes several plausible solutions to the same problem without giving the buyer a reliable reason to choose one over another.

Lean Practitioner and AI-Enabled Improvement Practitioner both promise improvement work with corporate AI, at the same displayed fee and teaching duration. Green Belt also includes AI and service design. “Bring the problem and we will name the method” postpones the distinction to a sales conversation.

There is also a substantive scope conflict: Certifications describes Lean Practitioner as designing and running a management system, assessed in place. Its course page sells an Action Workout and a group improvement initiative. Those are not interchangeable descriptions of capability.

**Why it costs:** An operations leader cannot confidently shortlist the right intervention. Procurement can compare prices but cannot tell whether it is comparing equivalent outcomes. The specialised AI family can also make the claim that AI is built into every programme harder to understand.

**What I would do:** Explain the boundaries using buyer decisions: starting problem, depth of analysis, work produced, assessment and when another route fits better. Resolve the Lean Practitioner scope first. Explain what someone gains or gives up by choosing the AI-specific programme over the other practitioner routes. Keep a separate AI family only if the actual offer supports that distinction.

Keep Zero Defects as the specialist family name for the audience described in the brief. Right First Time would soften the explicit regulated-quality intent without resolving any of these selection problems. Treat it as an outcome concept if useful, rather than forcing it over the specialist offer. This is a buyer-positioning judgement, not verification of standards compliance.

**7. The copy's contempt for weaker training occasionally damages its own credibility.**

**Where:** [Approach](https://6b959d5a-lmi-website.equibt.workers.dev/approach/), “Teach on the real problem” and “Finish the project”; [Government](https://6b959d5a-lmi-website.equibt.workers.dev/in-house/government/), hero and context; [About](https://6b959d5a-lmi-website.equibt.workers.dev/about/), group distinction.

**What is wrong:** Absolute, adversarial claims make the offer sound less considered than the practical teaching position underneath them.

Saying a simple case study teaches “the vocabulary and nothing else” dismisses a legitimate way to practise a method before applying it. It also sits awkwardly beside the site's positive simulation testimonial and Lean Practitioner's live simulation. Saying training without a closed project “has produced nothing” undermines the awareness, foundation and leadership products you also sell.

The government promise that improvement survives a ministerial change is stronger than the training provider can independently guarantee. Recognising approval chains is credible. Promising organisational durability without explaining sponsor ownership, handover and governance is not.

**Why it costs:** An experienced L&D buyer may feel their existing training choices are being sneered at. A government leader may read the strongest line as marketing overreach. Both reactions reduce willingness to trust the more defensible claims.

**What I would do:** Distinguish practising a method from demonstrating transfer into work. State the evidence appropriate to each course level. Explain what training contributes to continuity and what remains the organisation's responsibility. Remove unsupported competitor generalisations rather than trying to make them punchier.

The group relationship itself is credible: the two homepages share an emphasis on capability that lasts. However, EQUIBT also says training is central to its advisory work. Explain the difference through the engagement purchased and who owns delivery, rather than insisting on an absolute training-versus-consulting divide.

**8. The current visual system is not close enough to the intended explainer aesthetic.**

**Where:** [Methods](https://6b959d5a-lmi-website.equibt.workers.dev/methods/), large icon panels; [Certifications](https://6b959d5a-lmi-website.equibt.workers.dev/certifications/), separation diagram at mobile width; [Government](https://6b959d5a-lmi-website.equibt.workers.dev/in-house/government/), hero illustration slot.

**What is wrong:** The site borrows the reference's colours and rounded containers, but its typography, broad background bands and generic line icons produce a different overall appearance and often add decoration before explanation.

Your clarification makes close visual resemblance the target. On that basis, the present direction is too far away. Adding the missing illustrations will help, but will not resolve the differences in the surrounding design.

The reference earns its complexity: objects, arrows and containers explain a process. The Methods cards devote substantial space to icons before naming the method and its fit. At mobile width this increases scrolling between choices that should be compared. The certification separation diagram becomes a miniature desktop diagram on mobile, just where the responsibility split needs to be unmistakable.

The government illustration slot appears before the substantive explanation and programme choices on mobile. A generic sector scene would consume that space without adding buying information. The issue is the slot's job, not the absence of the illustration.

**Why it costs:** The scanning procurement buyer must work harder to compare options, and the mobile cohort buyer reaches decision support later. A polished diagram that cannot communicate its relationship at phone size fails its commercial purpose.

**What I would do:** Make method name and fit the first elements of comparison cards; reduce the space given to generic motifs. Recompose the certification relationship as a vertical explanation on mobile. Require large illustration slots to explain something relevant, or position them after the decision-critical material. Keep the real cohort and faculty photography slots: they can establish delivery reality and accountability.

For the closer visual treatment, use these specific rules:

- Keep the page ground predominantly plain off-white. Concentrate pale blue inside diagrams, object panels and connecting bands rather than repeatedly colouring whole sections.
- Build the original people illustrations around expressive drawn faces, visible variation in painted clothing, pale neutral skin and dark hair and shoes. Their human specificity matters as much as the blue palette.
- Give original technical objects blue value fills, restrained mottling, slightly irregular navy outlines and white internal separations. The current smooth line icons cannot carry that register by themselves.
- Use drawn capsule edges and navy header caps to organise real stages and relationships. Let pale blue bands connect those stages. Keep cyan tied to the element being explained and red tied to risk or failure.
- Reconsider Source Serif 4 for body text. It adds an editorial quality, but it is a conspicuous departure from the reference's sans-serif teaching voice. If close resemblance is the priority, I would use a compatible sans body within the existing size ladder. This is an explicit objection to the locked type decision under your clarified target. Montserrat can remain while weight, measure and emphasis are brought closer to the reference.

The homepage cohort sequence already has a pale flow band behind the cards in this build, despite the brief saying that band is absent. It is the strongest starting point for this closer direction. Grow original artwork and diagrams around that functional grammar. The supplied frames remain a comparison reference, not proposed website assets or image-generation inputs.

**The remaining known decisions**

Pricing: keep confirmed fees visible. For unconfirmed offers, neither a blank nor an internal pricing placeholder helps the buyer. Use a truthful quotation route and state what determines the quote. “By application” suggests selection or eligibility, which is a different promise from a fee supplied on enquiry. Remove the visible statement that pricing has not yet been set from the eventual public offer. Duration alone does not establish comparable assessment or coaching value.

Title tails: I would not spend this review's commercial priority on them. A neutral course-name-plus-full-brand convention is sensible housekeeping; whether and where a credential is earned matters much more. I have not audited metadata or search presentation.

**What not to break**

The group looks coherent, not merely colour-matched. Its shared belief in capability that survives the engagement gives the visual relationship a reason to exist. The homepage's belt-specific AI examples, the explicit training/assessment separation, the connected cohort sequence and the Green Belt mobile price/action bar are all useful foundations. Keep those foundations while strengthening the evidence, fixing their destinations and bringing the visual treatment much closer to the explainer reference.

**If I could change only one thing:** make the actual route from training to assessed workplace evidence to credential consistent and explicit everywhere. It is the central reason a serious buyer should pay Lean Methods Institute, and currently the site's own detail weakens it.
